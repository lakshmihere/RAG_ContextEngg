**Emirates**[a] is one of the two [flag carriers](/wiki/Flag_carrier "Flag carrier")[2] of the [United Arab Emirates](/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") (the other being [Etihad Airways](/wiki/Etihad_Airways "Etihad Airways")). Based in [Al Garhoud](/wiki/Al_Garhoud "Al Garhoud"), Dubai, the airline is a subsidiary of [The Emirates Group](/wiki/The_Emirates_Group "The Emirates Group"), which is owned by the [government of Dubai](/wiki/Government_of_Dubai "Government of Dubai")'s [Investment Corporation of Dubai](/wiki/Investment_Corporation_of_Dubai "Investment Corporation of Dubai").[3] It is the world's largest long haul airline[4] as well as the largest airline in the [Middle East](/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East"),[5] operating more than 3,600 flights per week from its hub at [Terminal 3](/wiki/Dubai_International_Airport_Terminal_3 "Dubai International Airport Terminal 3") of [Dubai International Airport](/wiki/Dubai_International_Airport "Dubai International Airport"). It operates in more than 150 cities in 80 countries across six continents on its fleet of over 250 aircraft.[6] Cargo operations are undertaken by [Emirates SkyCargo](/wiki/Emirates_SkyCargo "Emirates SkyCargo").[7]

Emirates is the world's [third-largest airline](/wiki/World%27s_largest_airlines#By_scheduled_passenger-miles_flown_\(millions\) "World's largest airlines") by scheduled revenue passenger-kilometers flown.[8] It is also the [second-largest](/wiki/World%27s_largest_airlines#By_scheduled_freight_ton-kilometers_\(millions\) "World's largest airlines") in terms of freight tonne-kilometers flown. 

During the mid 1980s, [Gulf Air](/wiki/Gulf_Air "Gulf Air") began to cut back its services to Dubai. As a result, Emirates was founded on 15 March 1985, with backing from Dubai's [royal family](/wiki/Al_Maktoum "Al Maktoum") and its first two aircraft provided by [Pakistan International Airlines](/wiki/Pakistan_International_Airlines "Pakistan International Airlines"). With $10 million in start-up capital, it was required to operate independently of government subsidies. [Pakistan International Airlines](/wiki/Pakistan_International_Airlines "Pakistan International Airlines") also provided free training facilities to Emirates cabin crew at [Karachi Airport](/wiki/Jinnah_International_Airport "Jinnah International Airport"). The airline was founded by [Ahmed bin Saeed Al Maktoum](/wiki/Ahmed_bin_Saeed_Al_Maktoum "Ahmed bin Saeed Al Maktoum"), the airline's present chairman. In the years following its founding, the airline rapidly expanded both its fleet and its destinations. In October 2008, Emirates moved all of its operations at Dubai International Airport to Terminal 3.[9]

Emirates operates a mixed fleet of [Airbus](/wiki/Airbus "Airbus") and [Boeing](/wiki/Boeing "Boeing") [wide-body aircraft](/wiki/Wide-body_aircraft "Wide-body aircraft") and is one of the few airlines to operate an all-wide-body aircraft fleet (excluding Emirates Executive).[10] As of January 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit), Emirates is the world's largest [Airbus A380](/wiki/Airbus_A380 "Airbus A380") operator with 116 aircraft in service.[11] Since its introduction, the Airbus A380 has become an integral part of the [Emirates fleet](/wiki/Emirates_fleet "Emirates fleet"), especially on long-haul, high-density routes. Emirates is also the world's largest [Boeing 777](/wiki/Boeing_777 "Boeing 777") operator with 133 aircraft in service.[12]

## History

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

Main article: [History of Emirates (airline)](/wiki/History_of_Emirates_\(airline\) "History of Emirates \(airline\)")

Emirates was founded in March 1985 with backing from Dubai's ruler, [Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum](/wiki/Mohammed_bin_Rashid_Al_Maktoum "Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum"). 

On 25 October 1985, Emirates operated its first flight from [Dubai International Airport](/wiki/Dubai_International_Airport "Dubai International Airport") to [Jinnah International Airport](/wiki/Jinnah_International_Airport "Jinnah International Airport") in Karachi, Pakistan using an [Airbus A300B4-200](/wiki/Airbus_A300B4-200 "Airbus A300B4-200"), registered AP-BBM. Later a second flight departed Dubai for [Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport](/wiki/Chhatrapati_Shivaji_Maharaj_International_Airport "Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport") in Mumbai, India, using a [Boeing 737-300](/wiki/Boeing_737-300 "Boeing 737-300"), registered AP-BCD, both [wet-leased](/wiki/Aircraft_lease "Aircraft lease") from [Pakistan International Airlines](/wiki/Pakistan_International_Airlines "Pakistan International Airlines"). In 1986, Emirates added [Ratmalana Airport](/wiki/Ratmalana_Airport "Ratmalana Airport"), [Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport](/wiki/Hazrat_Shahjalal_International_Airport "Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport"), [Queen Alia International Airport](/wiki/Queen_Alia_International_Airport "Queen Alia International Airport") and [Cairo International Airport](/wiki/Cairo_International_Airport "Cairo International Airport") to its route network. 

On 3 July 1987, A6-EKA flew from [Toulouse–Blagnac Airport](/wiki/Toulouse%E2%80%93Blagnac_Airport "Toulouse–Blagnac Airport") to [Dubai](/wiki/Dubai "Dubai") as Emirates took delivery of its first owned aircraft, an [Airbus A310-300](/wiki/Airbus_A310-300 "Airbus A310-300"). Flights to [Frankfurt Airport](/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport "Frankfurt Airport") commenced via [Atatürk Airport](/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk_Airport "Atatürk Airport"), [London Gatwick](/wiki/Gatwick_Airport "Gatwick Airport") and [Male International Airport](/wiki/Male_International_Airport "Male International Airport"). This was followed by [Changi Airport](/wiki/Changi_Airport "Changi Airport"), [Bangkok airport](/wiki/Don_Mueang_International_Airport "Don Mueang International Airport") and [Hong Kong International Airport](/wiki/Hong_Kong_International_Airport "Hong Kong International Airport"). 

During its early years, Emirates experienced strong growth, averaging 30% annually.[13] The [Gulf War](/wiki/Gulf_War "Gulf War") helped boost business for the airline as it was the only airline to continue flying in the last ten days of the war. In June 1991 shortly after the end of the hostilities caused by the Gulf War, Emirates finally managed to acquire slots at [London Heathrow](/wiki/Heathrow_Airport "Heathrow Airport"). In 1996, Emirates took delivery of its baseline [Boeing 777-200](/wiki/Boeing_777-200 "Boeing 777-200"), followed by the extended-range version in 1997 and in 1999 the [Airbus A330-200](/wiki/Airbus_A330-200 "Airbus A330-200") and [Boeing 777-300](/wiki/Boeing_777-300 "Boeing 777-300"). In 2000, the airline placed an order for a large number of aircraft, including the [Boeing 777-300ER](/wiki/Boeing_777-300ER "Boeing 777-300ER") and the [Airbus A380](/wiki/Airbus_A380 "Airbus A380"), and also launched its frequent flyer program, Skywards.[14]

Since then, the airline has continued to expand its fleet and network, with a focus on operating flights to anywhere in the world via Dubai and competing with other major airlines on international routes. Its growth has attracted criticism from other carriers, who claim that the airline has unfair advantages and have called for an end to [open-skies policies](/wiki/Freedoms_of_the_air "Freedoms of the air") with the UAE as a result.[15][16] In 2017, Emirates "renewed its aircraft buying spree" and agreed to buy a number of Boeing's 787 Dreamliners for $15.1 billion. [The _Wall Street Journal_](/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal") described the deal as a "painful loss" for Airbus.[17] In 2023, Emirates ordered $50 billion of Boeing jets with their sister airline, [flyDubai](/wiki/Flydubai "Flydubai") at the Dubai Airshow.[18] Emirates ordered 90 aircraft, including both versions of the new long-haul jet.[18]

In April 2024, Emirates announced its plan to relocate its hub to [Al Maktoum Airport](/wiki/Al_Maktoum_International_Airport "Al Maktoum International Airport") (DWC) when the new airport is fully completed.[19] In 2026, Emirates temporarily suspended operations due to the [Iran War](/wiki/2026_Iran_war "2026 Iran war"). 

## Corporate management

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Corporate management")]

[](/wiki/File:Emirates_employees_at_the_inaugural_flight_to_Brussels.jpg)Emirates [flight attendants](/wiki/Flight_attendant "Flight attendant")

The airline is a subsidiary of [The Emirates Group](/wiki/The_Emirates_Group "The Emirates Group"), which is a subsidiary of the Dubai government's investment company, [Investment Corporation of Dubai](/wiki/Investment_Corporation_of_Dubai "Investment Corporation of Dubai").[20][21][22] The airline has recorded a profit every year, except its second year, and the growth has never fallen below 20% a year. In its first 11 years, it doubled in size every 3.5 years and has every four years since.[23]

In 2015, Emirates paid [dividends](/wiki/Dividend "Dividend") worth [AED](/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_dirham "United Arab Emirates dirham") 2.6 billion ([US$](/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar")708 million), compared to AED 1 billion (US$272 million) in 2014.[24] The government has received AED 14.6 billion from Emirates since dividends started being paid in 1999 for having provided an initial start-up capital of US$10 million and an additional investment of about US$80 million at the time of the airline's inception.[25] The Dubai government is the sole owner of the company, but it does not invest any new money into it or interfere with the airline's operations.[23]

### Structure and employment

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Structure and employment")]

Main article: [Emirates subsidiaries](/wiki/Emirates_subsidiaries "Emirates subsidiaries")

Emirates has diversified into related industries and sectors, including airport services, engineering, catering, and [tour operator](/wiki/Tour_operator "Tour operator") operations. Emirates has seven subsidiaries and its parent company has more than 50.[26][27] At the end of the fiscal year on 31 March 2020, the company employed a total of 59,519 staff, of which 21,789 were cabin crew, 4,313 were flight deck crew, 3,316 were in engineering, 12,627 were listed as other, 5,376 employees were at overseas stations, and 12,098 were at subsidiary companies.[1]: 72 The Emirates Group employed a total of 105,730 employees.[1]: 184

Emirates provides its employees with benefits such as comprehensive health plans and paid maternity and sick leave. Another strategy employed by Emirates is to use profit sharing and merit pay as part of its competency-based approach to performance management.[28] In 2023 and 2024, the group awarded its employees hefty bonuses as their share of the company's profits earned in those years. In 2023, employees got 24 weeks of pay[29] as their bonus, and in 2024, they received 20 weeks of pay.[30] In 2025, Emirates Group awarded its employees a 22-week bonus following a record-breaking profit of AED 22.7 billion—an 18% increase over the prior year. 

### Environmental record

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Environmental record")]

The airline claims to have lower emissions than other airlines because its fleet has an average fuel burn of fewer than 4 liters for every 100 passenger–kilometers.[31] In 2023, the airline announced it would invest $200 million over three years to fund research and development regarding the reduction of fossil fuels in commercial aviation, including investing in the development of alternative fuel and energy solutions.[32]

## Branding

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Branding")]

[](/wiki/File:EA_GP7200.jpg)Emirates logo (in Arabic) painted on one of its [Airbus A380-800](/wiki/Airbus_A380 "Airbus A380")'s engines [](/wiki/File:EMIRatesB777.jpg)A [Boeing 777-300ER](/wiki/Boeing_777-300ER "Boeing 777-300ER") (painted in the [Expo 2020](/wiki/Expo_2020 "Expo 2020") orange livery) at [Sydney Airport](/wiki/Sydney_Airport "Sydney Airport")

In the 1990s, Emirates launched its first set of commercials all with the slogan "So be good to yourself, Fly Emirates". In 1999, it launched a rare A330-200 commercial with different pictures showing the aircraft painted in the original livery and the livery used from 1999 until 2023, which was launched a few months prior. 

Commercials reappeared beginning in 2002 and the airline adopted the slogan "Fly Emirates. Keep Discovering" in 2004. In the 2010s, Emirates utilized multiple slogans in its advertising including "Fly Emirates. Keep Discovering", "Fly Emirates To over Six Continents", and "Hello Tomorrow".[41] Emirates currently uses the slogan "Fly Better". 

Emirates introduced a new uniform design in August 2008 for its 16,000 staff, designed by Simon Jersey. The offboard uniform includes the Emirates hat, red kick-pleats in the skirts, more fitted blouses, and the return of red leather shoes and handbags. For the onboard uniform, male and female cabin crew wear service waistcoats in place of the previously worn service jackets and tabards. The male flight attendants wear a chocolate brown suit, featuring pinstripes, with a cream shirt and caramel, honey, and red tie. Both male and female pursers wear this chocolate brown color but with no red featured.[42]

Since its formation in 1985, Emirates aircraft have carried a section of the [United Arab Emirates](/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") flag on the tail fins, a [calligraphy](/wiki/Calligraphy "Calligraphy") version of the logo in Arabic on the engines, and the "Emirates" logo on the fuselage both in [Arabic](/wiki/Arabic "Arabic") and English. The color scheme used since 1985 was changed in November 1999, with the first Boeing 777-300.[43] This change included the modification of the logotype, the enlargement and movement of the English logo (the Arabic remaining smaller) towards the front of the aircraft, and a different, flowing flag on the tailfin.[44]

In 2022, Emirates launched two commercials featuring a flight attendant standing on the spire of the [Burj Khalifa](/wiki/Burj_Khalifa "Burj Khalifa"). The first commercial was about the UAE moving to the UK's Amber list in the wake of the COVID-19 travel restrictions. The second commercial was to promote the [Expo 2020](/wiki/Expo_2020 "Expo 2020") event with an Airbus A380, painted in a special livery, circling the woman. The woman in the videos was a qualified stuntwoman dressed as an Emirates flight attendant.[45]

## Sponsorship

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Sponsorship")]

### Infrastructure

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Infrastructure")]

[](/wiki/File:Emirates_Air_Line,_London_01-07-2012_\(7551144398\).jpg)[Emirates Air Line](/wiki/Emirates_Air_Line_\(cable_car\) "Emirates Air Line \(cable car\)") cable cars in [London](/wiki/London "London")

From 2011 until 2022, Emirates sponsored the [Emirates Air Line](/wiki/London_Cable_Car "London Cable Car") [cable car](/wiki/Aerial_lift "Aerial lift") over the [River Thames](/wiki/River_Thames "River Thames") in [East London](/wiki/East_London "East London").[46]

Since 2015, Emirates has sponsored the England-based [Spinnaker Tower](/wiki/Spinnaker_Tower "Spinnaker Tower") in [Portsmouth](/wiki/Portsmouth "Portsmouth") on the south coast.[47] The airline had £3.5 million worth of plans to paint the landmark red, but after discussion with the residents of Portsmouth and [Southsea](/wiki/Southsea "Southsea"), Emirates agreed the tower was to be colored blue and gold, with red lettering of the Emirates sponsor,[48] for the reason that [Portsmouth F.C.](/wiki/Portsmouth_F.C. "Portsmouth F.C.") (the local football team) is colored blue and rival football team [Southampton F.C.](/wiki/Southampton_F.C. "Southampton F.C.") is colored red. It is now named "Emirates Spinnaker Tower".

#### Cricket

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Cricket")]

[](/wiki/File:A6-EOH_A380_Emirates2.jpg)An A380 in a special livery for the [2019 Cricket World Cup](/wiki/2019_Cricket_World_Cup "2019 Cricket World Cup")

Emirates sponsors [Cricket Australia](/wiki/Cricket_Australia "Cricket Australia"),[49] Lord's Taverners,[50] and Pro Arch Tournament.[51] Its branding also features on international [cricket umpires](/wiki/Umpire_\(cricket\) "Umpire \(cricket\)")' shirts.[52] Emirates was also an official partner of the [International Cricket Council](/wiki/International_Cricket_Council "International Cricket Council"). The deal gives Emirates association with all major ICC tournaments, including the 2011, 2015, and 2019 [ICC](/wiki/International_Cricket_Council "International Cricket Council") Cricket World Cups, the Women's Cricket World Cups, ICC Champions Trophy, and ICC World Twenty20.[53]

Emirates is the Twenty20 shirt sponsor of [Durham County Cricket Club](/wiki/Durham_County_Cricket_Club "Durham County Cricket Club") and holds the naming rights to the Riverside Ground, now known as [Emirates Riverside](/wiki/Emirates_Riverside "Emirates Riverside"), as well as the naming rights to the [Emirates Old Trafford Cricket Ground](/wiki/Old_Trafford_Cricket_Ground "Old Trafford Cricket Ground"), and is the shirt sponsor of [Lancashire County Cricket Club](/wiki/Lancashire_County_Cricket_Club "Lancashire County Cricket Club"). Emirates was also the major sponsor of the [Kings XI Punjab](/wiki/Kings_XI_Punjab "Kings XI Punjab") (seasons two-four) and [Deccan Chargers](/wiki/Deccan_Chargers "Deccan Chargers") (season five), teams of the [Indian Premier League](/wiki/Indian_Premier_League "Indian Premier League"), the largest domestic cricket tournament in the world.

#### Rugby

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Rugby")]

Emirates has sponsored the [Super League](/wiki/Super_League "Super League") Rugby League team, the [Warrington Wolves](/wiki/Warrington_Wolves "Warrington Wolves") between 2013 and 2017. The multi-year sponsorship cost has been touted as around £300,000 annually.[61]

It is also the main sponsor of [USA Rugby](/wiki/USA_Rugby "USA Rugby")[62] and the [World Rugby](/wiki/World_Rugby "World Rugby") panel of international referees.[62] Since 2015, Emirates has sponsored the South African [Super Rugby](/wiki/Super_Rugby "Super Rugby") team, the [Lions](/wiki/Lions_\(Super_Rugby\) "Lions \(Super Rugby\)") and has the naming rights of the team and the [Ellis Park](/wiki/Ellis_Park_Stadium "Ellis Park Stadium") rugby stadium. 

It also sponsors the [Rugby World Cup](/wiki/Rugby_World_Cup "Rugby World Cup") since 2007 and the [Women's Rugby World Cup](/wiki/Women%27s_Rugby_World_Cup "Women's Rugby World Cup") starting in 2025.

On 23 September 2019, Emirates partnered with [Beirut Basketball Club](/wiki/Beirut_Club "Beirut Club") to sponsor their 2019–2020 season; the deal included branding opportunities during televised matches, social media activation rights, and game ticket allocations.[63] The season was later canceled amidst the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Lebanon "COVID-19 pandemic in Lebanon"). This deal also included getting the naming rights for the [NBA Cup](/wiki/NBA_Cup "NBA Cup"), becoming the Emirates NBA Cup starting in the 2024 season. Additionally, an Emirates patch would be added to NBA referee jerseys.[65]

## Network

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Network")]

Main article: [List of Emirates destinations](/wiki/List_of_Emirates_destinations "List of Emirates destinations")

[](/wiki/File:Emirates_A6-EWA_Boeing_777-200LR_taking_off_from_LAX_\(5222343985\).jpg)A Boeing 777-200LR taking off from [Los Angeles (LAX)](/wiki/Los_Angeles_International_Airport "Los Angeles International Airport"), one of the airline's longest nonstop flights [](/wiki/File:Airbus_A380-842_\(cn_272,_A6-EVS\)_2022-06-21_Andre_Gerwing_Collection_ID_002123.jpg)A6-EVS, the final [Airbus A380](/wiki/Airbus_A380 "Airbus A380") produced

As of August 2024, Emirates operates over 3,000 flights every week across its network of 137 destinations in 77 countries across six continents from its hub in Dubai.[73] On 21 November 2024, Emirates added a 5th weekly flight to its Dubai-[Antananarivo](/wiki/Antananarivo "Antananarivo") route, commencing on 13 December 2024 for a duration of 4 weeks, to cater for the surge in holidaymakers during the year-end festive season. A third daily flight was added for [Mauritius](/wiki/Sir_Seewoosagur_Ramgoolam_International_Airport "Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam International Airport") on 1st December 2025 supplementing the two daily flights operated by the [Airbus A380](/wiki/Airbus_A380 "Airbus A380"). The move saw an increase in capacity for the number of seats to [Seychelles](/wiki/Seychelles "Seychelles") and [Madagascar](/wiki/Madagascar "Madagascar").[74]

On 10 October 2024, it was reported that Emirates had relaunched direct flights to [Adelaide Airport](/wiki/Adelaide_Airport "Adelaide Airport") beginning 28 October 2024. The flight resumption to Adelaide was a fourth option for travelers to the [United Kingdom](/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") or [Europe](/wiki/Europe "Europe") after [Malaysia Airlines](/wiki/Malaysia_Airlines "Malaysia Airlines"), [Singapore Airlines](/wiki/Singapore_Airlines "Singapore Airlines") and [Qatar Airways](/wiki/Qatar_Airways "Qatar Airways").[75]

In March 2025, Emirates announced plans to expand its Asian network by introducing flights to three new destinations: [Shenzhen](/wiki/Shenzhen "Shenzhen") in [China](/wiki/China "China"), [Da Nang](/wiki/Da_Nang "Da Nang") in [Vietnam](/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam"), and [Siem Reap](/wiki/Siem_Reap "Siem Reap") in [Cambodia](/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia"). This expansion marks Emirates' fourth gateway into the Chinese mainland and its third into Vietnam. With these additions, the Dubai-based airline will now serve 49 destinations across Asia and the Pacific. This move aims to enhance connectivity and cater to the growing demand for travel in these regions.[76][77]

### Alliance

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Alliance")]

Emirates has partnerships with other airlines, but is not a member of any of the three global [airline alliances](/wiki/Airline_alliance "Airline alliance") – [Oneworld](/wiki/Oneworld "Oneworld"), [SkyTeam](/wiki/SkyTeam "SkyTeam"), or [Star Alliance](/wiki/Star_Alliance "Star Alliance"). In 2000, the airline briefly considered joining Star Alliance, but opted to remain independent.[78] The reasoning for this was later revealed by the senior vice president of the airline's commercial operations worldwide: "Your ability to react in the marketplace is hindered because you need a consensus from your alliance partners".[79]

## Divisions

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Divisions")]

### Emirates Executive

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Emirates Executive")]

Emirates Executive was launched in 2013 for corporate and private charters. It operates a single [Airbus ACJ319](/wiki/Airbus_Corporate_Jets "Airbus Corporate Jets") business jet,[126] accommodating 19 people.[127] It features a mix of private suites and seating, a lounge, a dining area, and bathrooms with full-height showers.[128]

### Emirates SkyCargo

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Emirates SkyCargo")]

[](/wiki/File:A6-EFV_STN_140825.jpg)An Emirates SkyCargo [Boeing 777F](/wiki/Boeing_777F "Boeing 777F") arriving at [London Stansted Airport](/wiki/London_Stansted_Airport "London Stansted Airport")

[Emirates SkyCargo](/wiki/Emirates_SkyCargo "Emirates SkyCargo") is the cargo division of Emirates. It began operations in October 1985, the same year Emirates was formed, and launched its aircraft services in 2001 with a Boeing 747 Freighter. It serves 10 exclusive cargo destinations, besides others in common with the Emirates passenger network.[129] During the 2020 pandemic, SkyCargo also began to operate [777-300ER](/wiki/777-300ER "777-300ER") and [A380](/wiki/Airbus_A380 "Airbus A380") passenger aircraft as [preighters](/wiki/Preighter "Preighter") to expand their total cargo capacity.[130] In 2022, Emirates ordered 5 more [Boeing 777](/wiki/Boeing_777 "Boeing 777") freighters, up from their 11 at the time.[131]

## Fleet

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Fleet")]

Main article: [Emirates fleet](/wiki/Emirates_fleet "Emirates fleet")

[](/wiki/File:Hamburg_Airport_Emirates_Boeing_777-31HER_A6-ENV_\(DSC05111\).jpg)A [Boeing 777-300ER](/wiki/Boeing_777-300ER "Boeing 777-300ER") at [Hamburg Airport](/wiki/Hamburg_Airport "Hamburg Airport") [](/wiki/File:Emirates_A350-900_Bologna_Airport_-_2025.jpg)An [Airbus A350](/wiki/Airbus_A350 "Airbus A350") at [Bologna Airport](/wiki/Bologna_Airport "Bologna Airport")

As of March 2026, Emirates operates a fleet of 261 passenger aircraft and 13 cargo aircraft operated by Emirates SkyCargo.[132] Emirates currently has 116 [Airbus A380s](/wiki/Airbus_A380 "Airbus A380") and 128 [Boeing 777s](/wiki/Boeing_777 "Boeing 777"), including 10 [777-200LRs](/wiki/Boeing_777-200LR "Boeing 777-200LR") and 118 [777-300ERs](/wiki/Boeing_777-300ER "Boeing 777-300ER"), making them the largest operator of both types.[133] The airline also has 17 [Airbus A350-900s](/wiki/Airbus_A350-900 "Airbus A350-900"), and one [Airbus A319](/wiki/Airbus_A319 "Airbus A319") as an [executive jet](/wiki/Executive_jet "Executive jet") (this is painted in a plain white livery). Emirates has had no [narrow-body aircraft](/wiki/Narrow-body_aircraft "Narrow-body aircraft") in its mainline fleet since 1995. 

In July 2014, Emirates finalized an order for 150 [Boeing 777X](/wiki/Boeing_777X "Boeing 777X") aircraft, consisting of 35 777-8s and 115 777-9s,[134] and was expected to become the launch operator for the 777X in mid 2020.[135] In November 2017, it signed a commitment for 40 787-10s,[136] but by early 2019, it was considering cancelling this order because engine margins were insufficient for the hot Dubai weather, in favour of the Airbus A350.[137]

In February 2019, Emirates signed a memorandum of understanding with Airbus for 40 [A330-900s](/wiki/Airbus_A330-900 "Airbus A330-900") and 30 [A350-900s](/wiki/Airbus_A350-900 "Airbus A350-900"), while reducing its total A350 order to eight[138] (with the last one to be delivered in 2022)[139] after which Airbus planned to permanently cease production of the A380. Emirates received the final A380 (registration A6-EVS) built by Airbus on 16 December 2021. It was the 123rd A380 to join the fleet. The delivery officially marked the end of the Airbus A380 production 14 years after the first delivery to [Singapore Airlines](/wiki/Singapore_Airlines "Singapore Airlines") in 2007.[140]

In November 2019, Emirates announced an order of 50 A350-900s worth US$16 billion that superseded the February memorandum of understanding.[141] Also in November 2019, Emirates placed an order for 30 Boeing 787-9 [Dreamliners](/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner "Boeing 787 Dreamliner") for a value of US$8.8 billion, while reducing its order of 777Xs from 150 to 126.[142]

In December 2019, Emirates clarified that 11 of its 777X orders were subject to [reconfirmation](/wiki/Reconfirmation "Reconfirmation"), but the overall number of orders had not been reduced.[143] Being the largest operator of the A380, Emirates maintains its fleet via the [MRO](/wiki/Maintenance_\(technical\) "Maintenance \(technical\)") subsidiary of Safran, [OEMServices](/wiki/OEMServices "OEMServices").[144]

On 13 November 2023, at the [Dubai Airshow](/wiki/Dubai_Airshow "Dubai Airshow"), Emirates finalized an order of 90 777X aircraft worth US$52 Billion, including 50 Boeing 777-9 aircraft and 35 Boeing 777-8 aircraft. This brings the total Boeing 777X backlog to 205 aircraft. Another 5 Boeing 787 Dreamliners were ordered, growing Emirates' 787 backlog to 35 – while converting 30 787-9s to 20 787-8 and 10 787-10 aircraft.[145]

On 16 November 2023, also at the Dubai Airshow, Emirates ordered an additional 15 Airbus A350-900s worth US$6 billion, bringing the total of A350-900s ordered by Emirates to 65.[146] The first A350-900 was delivered on November 25, 2024.[147] A350 services began on 4 January 2025, with an inaugural flight to [Edinburgh](/wiki/Edinburgh "Edinburgh"). 

On 18 April 2025, it was announced that Emirates would be receiving [Airbus A350-900](/wiki/Airbus_A350 "Airbus A350") outfitted with seating for ultra long haul flights, capable of flying over 15 hours non-stop with a range of over 14,000 km (8,699 mi; 7,559 nmi). [Adelaide](/wiki/Adelaide "Adelaide") will be the first city served, replacing the current [Boeing 777-200LR](/wiki/Boeing_777-200LR "Boeing 777-200LR") aircraft serving the city.[148]

On 19 November 2025, at the Dubai Airshow 2025, Emirates announced a top-up order for 8 additional [A350-900](/wiki/Airbus_A350 "Airbus A350") aircraft worth US$3.4 billion, bringing the total Emirates A350-900 order to 73 with 13 already delivered at the time.[3]

## Livery

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Livery")]

### First livery (1985–1999)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: First livery \(1985–1999\)")]

[](/wiki/File:Emirates_Airbus_A300_Bidini.jpg)A now-retired Airbus A300-600R painted in the airline's first livery

The first livery of Emirates, created by Negus & Negus, was similar to the second livery, except that the company name "Emirates" was written in a different font; it was relatively smaller, located on the top of the windows; and it was followed by the company name in Arabic. All aircraft wearing the first-generation livery were either repainted or retired. This livery was retired by 2005 as the last aircraft with the first generation livery (an Airbus A310-300) was repainted to the second generation livery. 

### Second livery (1999–2023)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Second livery \(1999–2023\)")]

[](/wiki/File:Emirates_Boeing_777_A6-ECS_Perth_2021_\(01\).jpg)A Boeing 777-300ER painted in the airline's second livery

The second Emirates livery, which featured a [UAE flag](/wiki/Flag_of_the_United_Arab_Emirates "Flag of the United Arab Emirates") on the vertical stabilizer and a white fuselage, with the golden word "Emirates" painted on the upper fuselage, was introduced in November 1999 on the Boeing 777-300 and the Airbus A330-200, as well as all other aircraft that were delivered from November 1999 onward. The livery rolled out shortly after in 2000 on the rest of the Emirates fleet, and Emirates repainted all aircraft to this livery by 2005. The second Emirates livery also kept the Arabic company name, but the font size was smaller than the one from the first Emirates livery. The Emirates logo in Arabic is painted gold on all engines. The livery was updated in 2005 when the red word "Emirates" was introduced and painted on the belly of the fuselage. 

### Current livery (2023–present)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: Current livery \(2023–present\)")]

[](/wiki/File:Emirates_Airbus_A380_A6-EOE_Perth_2024_\(01\).jpg)An Airbus A380 painted in the current livery

On 16 March 2023, Emirates revealed its new livery. The livery features a more dynamic, flowing design of the UAE flag on the tailfin with a 3D effect. The wingtips are now painted red, displaying the Emirates logo in white Arabic calligraphy. Passengers with window seats can see the UAE flag colors painted on the inside of the wingtips, facing the fuselage.[149]

The airline's [Airbus A350s](/wiki/Airbus_A350 "Airbus A350") will also include the updated UAE flag on the inner side of the winglets.

### Cabin

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=30 "Edit section: Cabin")]

First class

The two types of [first class](/wiki/First_class_\(aviation\) "First class \(aviation\)") seating are the fully enclosed suite with a floor-to-ceiling door and a private suite with doors that close but do not extend to the ceiling. Both suites come complete with closing doors to ensure privacy, a [minibar](/wiki/Minibar "Minibar"), a coat rack, and storage. They also feature the ICE [in-flight entertainment](/wiki/In-flight_entertainment "In-flight entertainment") system on a 23-inch-wide (58 cm) [LCD screen](/wiki/Liquid_crystal_display "Liquid crystal display") in the private suites and a 32-inch-wide (81 cm) on the fully enclosed suite. The seat converts into a 2-metre-long (79 in) fully flat bed. Private suites are available on three-class and four-class Airbus A380-800 and three-class Boeing 777-300ER aircraft.[150] The fully enclosed suites are available only on newly delivered Boeing 777-300ER aircraft.[151]

On its newly delivered Airbus A380-800, first class features private suites,[152] two shower-equipped lavatories and a spa,[153] and access to the first/business class bar area and lounge.[154] Premium class seating is located on the entire upper deck of the aircraft. 

Emirates introduced a new first-class cabin for its Boeing 777-300ER fleet on 12 November 2017[155] and first flights to Brussels and Geneva on 1 December 2017. The new first-class cabin is configured with six suites on a 1-1-1 layout. Both of the middle suites are equipped with three virtual windows, which are high-definition LCD screens that relay real-time images using HD cameras on either side of the aircraft. Amenities include two minibars placed on either side of the entertainment screen, a 13-inch tablet with a front camera to communicate with the cabin crew and to order room service, and a panel to control the lighting and temperature inside the suite. Emirates has also introduced a new seat in collaboration with [Mercedes-Benz](/wiki/Mercedes-Benz "Mercedes-Benz"), which features a new zero-gravity position.[156][157] The suites are expected to resemble "a private bedroom on a luxury yacht".[158]

Business class

[Business class](/wiki/Business_class "Business class") on Boeing 777-200LRs and Boeing 777-300ERs feature seats with a 1.5-metre-long (60 in) pitch that reclines to 2-metre-long (79 in), angled lie-flat beds.[159] Amenities include a massage function, privacy partition, winged headrest with six-way movement, two individual reading lights, and an overhead light per seat; in-seat power supply, USB ports, and an RCA socket for laptop connection; and over 600 channels of entertainment on the ICE system, shown on a 23 in-wide (58 cm) HD TV screen.[160]

On Airbus A380-800 aircraft,[161] the seats recline to form a fully flat bed and are equipped with personal minibars. The unique staggered layout makes half of the business-class seats on Emirates A380[162] 23 cm (9 in) shorter than the others, at only 1.8 m (70 in) long.[163] Business class passengers also have access to an on-board bar at the rear of the aircraft.[159][164]

Premium economy class

In December 2020, it was announced that Emirates' new Premium economy cabin would be equipped with [Recaro](/wiki/Recaro "Recaro") PL3530 seats which were designed exclusively for the airline.[165]

The seats offer a pitch of up to 40 in (101 cm), a recline of 8 in (20 cm) and measure 19.5 in (49 cm) wide. All seats are equipped with a 13.3 in (33 cm) entertainment screen using the Emirates ICE system.[166]

Currently, 22 Airbus A380 aircraft in the Emirates fleet have the new premium economy class cabin. These seats are also set to be retrofitted on the airline's older Boeing 777-300ERs and Airbus A380s as part of a retrofit program of US$2 billion that began at the end of 2022; by the end of the program, 67 Airbus A380s and 53 Boeing 777s will be fitted with premium economy.[167][168]

Economy class

Emirates [economy class](/wiki/Economy_class "Economy class") offers a 79–81-centimetre-long (31–32 in) [seat pitch](/wiki/Seat_pitch "Seat pitch") on Airbus aircraft and 86 cm (34 in) on Boeing aircraft, with standard seat width (except on the Boeing 777 fleet). Emirates has 10 seats per row on its Boeing 777 fleet. The seat features adjustable headrests, a 3000-channel ICE system, and in-seat laptop power outlets on newer aircraft and laptop recharging facilities in galleys in older aircraft. Additional recline is available on A380 economy-class seats.[169][170][171]

### Catering

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=31 "Edit section: Catering")]

[](/wiki/File:Emirates_Business_class_meal_on_A380_\(5646674365\).jpg)An appetizer served in business class on Emirates [](/wiki/File:Emirates_economy_class_dinner.jpg)An on-board meal served in economy class

Catering on Emirates flights from [Dubai International](/wiki/Dubai_International_Airport "Dubai International Airport") is provided by [Emirates Flight Catering](/wiki/Emirates_Flight_Catering "Emirates Flight Catering"), which operates one of the largest airline catering facilities in the world.[172] Emirates also offers special meal options, in all classes, based on age, dietary restrictions and preference, and religious observance. Special meals must be ordered in advance at least 24 hours before the flight departure time. All meals are prepared according to Halal dietary guidelines.[173] In June 2018, Emirates signed a $40 million joint venture with [Oakland](/wiki/Oakland,_California "Oakland, California")-based Crop One Holdings, to build and maintain the world's largest hydroponic growing facility. It would provide daily yields of roughly 3 tons of leafy greens per day to all flights, with a near 150,000-square-foot (14,000 m2) indoor, vertical farm.[174]

### In-flight entertainment system (IFE)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=32 "Edit section: In-flight entertainment system \(IFE\)")]

Emirates became one of the first airlines in the world to introduce a personal entertainment system on commercial aircraft in 1992, with [Virgin Atlantic](/wiki/Virgin_Atlantic "Virgin Atlantic") introducing a similar system throughout all cabins of its aircraft.[175] All three classes feature a personal IFE system in the Emirates aircraft. There are two types of entertainment systems in Emirates: ICE and ICE Digital Widescreen. 

In 2012, Emirates introduced larger high-definition IFE screens in all classes. The new IFE is the first to be fully high definition, and in economy, the screens are the largest offered by any airline. The new IFE will only be installed on the Airbus A380 fleet and the newly delivered Boeing 777s.[176]

#### In-flight entertainment system (ICE)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=33 "Edit section: In-flight entertainment system \(ICE\)")]

[](/wiki/File:Emirates_A380_economy_ITB_2017_\(2\).JPG)An Emirates economy class seat equipped with the ICE (information, communication, entertainment) in-flight entertainment system

ICE (information, communication, entertainment) is the In-Flight Entertainment (IFE) system operated by Emirates. 

Introduced in 2003, ICE is available on all new aircraft and now features 4,000 channels (on most flights) for all passengers.[177] ICE is found on the airline's Airbus A350-900, Airbus A380-800, Boeing 777-200LR, and Boeing 777-300ER.[178]

In July 2007, Emirates introduced ICE Digital Widescreen, an updated version of ICE. It offered over 1200 channels of selected entertainment to all passengers. ICE Digital Widescreen is available on all Emirates aircraft.[179]

In 2015, Emirates upgraded ICE to the new eX3 version, which included new upgrades that improved the passenger experience, such as a handset with more controls, larger screens, new sockets, some 3,500 channels of movies, TV shows, music, and games on-demand and in multiple languages, new ICE features, such as a Voyager app, Bluetooth audio, and personal video playback. This is fitted on all B777 and A380 aircraft delivered after 2009.[180][181] The redesigned version, based on Thales's AVANT Up, was introduced in 2025 with its first A350 delivery.[182]

According to Emirates, ICE has received more awards than any other airline in the world for inflight entertainment.[183]

Information

The system is based on the 3000i system from [Panasonic Avionics Corporation](/wiki/Panasonic_Avionics_Corporation "Panasonic Avionics Corporation"). ICE provides passengers with a direct data link to [BBC News](/wiki/BBC_News "BBC News"). ICE is the first IFE system to be connected directly to automatic news updates. This is complemented by ICE's _Airshow_ moving-map software from [Rockwell Collins](/wiki/Rockwell_Collins "Rockwell Collins"). Exterior cameras located on the aircraft can be viewed by any passenger through the IFE system during takeoff, cruise, and landing. Emirates was also one of the first airlines to introduce a high-speed, in-flight internet service along with Singapore Airlines, by installing the [Inmarsat](/wiki/Inmarsat "Inmarsat")'s [satellite](/wiki/Satellite "Satellite") system and became the second airline in the world to offer live international television broadcasts using the same system.[184]

Communication

ICE has a link to an in-flight [email server](/wiki/Mail_transfer_agent "Mail transfer agent"), which allows passengers to access, send, or receive emails for [US$](/wiki/US$ "US$")1 per message.[185] ICE also supports a seat-to-seat chat service. In November 2006, the airline signed a deal with mobile communications firm [AeroMobile](/wiki/AeroMobile "AeroMobile") to allow [in-flight use of mobile phones](/wiki/Mobile_phones_on_aircraft "Mobile phones on aircraft") to call or [text](/wiki/Text_messaging "Text messaging") people on the ground. The service was first introduced in March 2008.[186]

Entertainment

The ICE system includes movies, music, and video games. ICE offers over 600 [on-demand](/wiki/Video_on_demand "Video on demand") movie titles, over 2000 [video on demand](/wiki/Video_on_demand "Video on demand") and prerecorded [television](/wiki/Television "Television") channels, over 1000 hours of music, and over 100 video game titles. ICE can be accessed in more than 40 languages, including English, French, German, Russian, Spanish, Arabic, Chinese, Hindi, Urdu, [Persian](/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language"), Korean, Tamil, Thai, Dutch, Swedish, Italian, and Japanese.[187] Since 2003, all entertainment options are available on demand to all classes with options to pause, forward, and rewind them. 

Emirates began to offer docking capability for [Apple Inc.](/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.")'s [iPod](/wiki/IPod "IPod") portable music and video player in mid 2007. This enabled the device's [battery](/wiki/Battery_\(electricity\) "Battery \(electricity\)") to be charged and integrated with ICE, which could then play music, television shows, or movies stored on the iPod and function as a control system. This feature was removed from Emirates aircraft starting in the late 2010s due to the iPod being discontinued.[188]

## Business model

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=34 "Edit section: Business model")]

[](/wiki/File:DXB_on_23_September_2007_Pict_5.jpg)Emirates aircraft parked at Dubai International Airport

Main article: [Emirates business model](/wiki/Emirates_business_model "Emirates business model")

Established network carriers in Europe and Australia, such as [Air France-KLM](/wiki/Air_France-KLM "Air France-KLM"), [British Airways](/wiki/British_Airways "British Airways"), [Lufthansa](/wiki/Lufthansa "Lufthansa"), and [Qantas](/wiki/Qantas "Qantas"), see Emirates' strategic decision to reposition itself as a global carrier as a major threat because it enables travelers to bypass traditional airline hubs such as London-Heathrow, Paris-CDG, and Frankfurt on their way between Europe/North America and Asia/Australia by changing flights in Dubai instead. These carriers also find it difficult to deal with the growing competitive threat Emirates poses to their business because of their much higher cost base.[189][190] Some of these carriers, notably Air France and Qantas, have accused Emirates of receiving hidden state subsidies and maintaining too close of a relationship with Dubai's airport authority and its aviation authority, both of which are also wholly state-owned entities that share the same government owner with the airline. Qantas' [chairman](/wiki/Leigh_Clifford "Leigh Clifford") claimed that Emirates can reduce its borrowing costs below market rates by taking advantage of its government shareholders' sovereign borrower status.[23] Emirates' president disagrees and has also referred to United States airlines bankruptcy protection as being a tangible form of state assistance. The airline makes regular profits.[191] In 2016, [American Airlines](/wiki/American_Airlines "American Airlines"), [Delta Air Lines](/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines "Delta Air Lines"), and [United Airlines](/wiki/United_Airlines "United Airlines") made similar claims, as well as stating that Emirates violates [Open Skies](/wiki/Open_Skies "Open Skies"), but these conflicts were resolved in May 2018.[192][193]

In May 2010, Emirates executives denied claims that the carrier does not pay taxes and receives substantial financial assistance from the Dubai government. They claimed that the airline received $80m in cash in the 25 years since the airline was established and this was substantially lower than what other national carriers had received. [Maurice Flanagan](/wiki/Maurice_Flanagan "Maurice Flanagan") also claimed that Emirates incurred social costs of around $600m in 2009, and this included municipal taxes to the city of Dubai. The airline also paid a dividend of AED 956m (US$260m) in 2010, compared to AED2.9bn ($793m) in 2009, and each year the Government has received at least $100m in dividends.[194] Emirates also faces competition from other Middle Eastern airlines, mainly [Qatar Airways](/wiki/Qatar_Airways "Qatar Airways") and [Abu Dhabi](/wiki/Abu_Dhabi "Abu Dhabi")–based [Etihad Airways](/wiki/Etihad_Airways "Etihad Airways").[195]

## Sustainability

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=35 "Edit section: Sustainability")]

In its efforts to reduce carbon emissions, the Emirates started exploring the use of sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) for its fleet since 2017. The airline has partnered with the world's leading biodiesel producers such as [Neste](/wiki/Neste "Neste"), a Finnish producer of sustainable aviation fuel, as well as [Shell](/wiki/Shell_plc "Shell plc") Aviation. In January 2023, the airline conducted a successful demonstration flight of a Boeing 777-300ER using 100 percent SAF in one of its two engines. In November of the same year, Emirates conducted another demonstration flight of an Airbus A380 using 100 percent SAF in one of the airplane's four engines.[196][197] Following successful tests, Emirates operated its first commercial flight using SAF blend on October 24, 2023, on Emirates flight EK 412 from Dubai to Sydney, Australia using an Airbus A380.[198]

Currently, the airline has suppliers set up to supply its flights with SAF in several airports around the world including Paris, Lyon, Oslo, Amsterdam, London, Singapore and its home base in Dubai.[199][200][201][202][203][204][205][206][207]

## Accidents and incidents

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=36 "Edit section: Accidents and incidents")]

Emirates has experienced several aircraft incidents (none with passenger or crew fatalities) 

  * On 9 April 2004, Emirates Flight 764, an [Airbus A340-300](/wiki/Airbus_A340-300 "Airbus A340-300") operating from [Johannesburg](/wiki/O._R._Tambo_International_Airport "O. R. Tambo International Airport") to Dubai, sustained serious damage during takeoff when it overran runway 03L, striking runway 21R [approach lights](/wiki/Approach_lighting_system "Approach lighting system"), causing four tires to burst, which threw debris into various parts of the aircraft, ultimately damaging the [flap](/wiki/Flap_\(aircraft\) "Flap \(aircraft\)") drive mechanism. This rendered the flaps immovable in the takeoff position. The aircraft returned for an emergency landing during which the normal braking system failed as a result of the damage. The aircraft was brought to a stop only 250 m (820 ft) from the end of the 3,400-metre (11,200 ft) runway using [reverse thrust](/wiki/Reverse_thrust "Reverse thrust") and the alternative braking system.[208][209] In their report, South African investigators found that the captain had used a wrong take-off technique, and criticized Emirates' training and rostering practices.[210]
  * On 20 March 2009, [Emirates Flight 407](/wiki/Emirates_Flight_407 "Emirates Flight 407"), an [Airbus A340-500](/wiki/Airbus_A340-500 "Airbus A340-500") registered A6-ERG en route from [Melbourne](/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne") to Dubai, failed to take off properly at [Melbourne Airport](/wiki/Melbourne_Airport "Melbourne Airport"), hitting several structures at the end of the runway before eventually climbing enough to return to the airport for a safe landing. There were no injuries, but the incident was severe enough to be classified as an accident by the [Australian Transport Safety Bureau](/wiki/Australian_Transport_Safety_Bureau "Australian Transport Safety Bureau").[211]
  * On 3 August 2016, [Emirates Flight 521](/wiki/Emirates_Flight_521 "Emirates Flight 521"), a [Boeing 777-300](/wiki/Boeing_777-300 "Boeing 777-300") registered A6-EMW and arriving from [Trivandrum International Airport](/wiki/Trivandrum_International_Airport "Trivandrum International Airport") crash-landed and caught fire at Dubai International Airport at 12:44 pm local time. All 282 passengers and 18 crew on board survived the impact with 32 injuries (4 seriously) reported. However, an airport firefighter died fighting the blaze. The aircraft was destroyed by the fire.[212] Flight 521 was the first [hull loss](/wiki/Hull_loss "Hull loss") in the history of Emirates.
  * On 14 April 2020, an Emirates [Boeing 777-300ER](/wiki/Boeing_777-300ER "Boeing 777-300ER") registered A6-EBR was struck by a [British Airways](/wiki/British_Airways "British Airways") [Airbus A350-1000](/wiki/Airbus_A350-1000 "Airbus A350-1000") (G-XWBA) on the ground while the A350 was pushing back from the gate for departure. No casualties were reported, however, the [horizontal stabilizers](/wiki/Stabilizer_\(aeronautics\) "Stabilizer \(aeronautics\)") on both aircraft were damaged as a result of the collision.
  * On 20 December 2021, Emirates Flight 231, a [Boeing 777-300ER](/wiki/Boeing_777-300ER "Boeing 777-300ER") registered as A6-EQI, departing Dubai International Airport towards [Washington Dulles](/wiki/Dulles_International_Airport "Dulles International Airport"), nearly overran the runway during takeoff, flying at only 75 ft (23 m) over houses located near the airport.[_[citation needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_][213][214] The aircraft was not damaged and there were no injuries.[215] The incident remains under investigation.[216][217]
  * On 1 July 2022, Emirates Flight 430, an [Airbus A380-842](/wiki/Airbus_A380-800 "Airbus A380-800") registered as A6-EVK departed Dubai International Airport towards [Brisbane Airport](/wiki/Brisbane_Airport "Brisbane Airport"). During the cruise, one of the aircraft's 22 tires experienced a rupture, which caused damage to a portion of the aerodynamic fairing. The plane landed safely in Brisbane and there were no fatalities.[218]
  * On 27 March 2024, an [Airbus A380](/wiki/Airbus_A380 "Airbus A380") suffered serious damage after it was hit by an emergency vehicle on the tarmac of [Moscow Domodedovo International Airport](/wiki/Moscow_Domodedovo_Airport "Moscow Domodedovo Airport").[219]
  * On 28 March 2024, an Emirates [Boeing 777](/wiki/Boeing_777 "Boeing 777") narrowly avoided a collision with an [Ethiopian Airlines](/wiki/Ethiopian_Airlines "Ethiopian Airlines") [Boeing 737 MAX](/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX "Boeing 737 MAX") over [Somaliland](/wiki/Somaliland "Somaliland").[220]
  * On 20 May 2024, Emirates Flight 508, a [Boeing 777-300ER](/wiki/Boeing_777-300ER "Boeing 777-300ER"), suffered a [bird strike](/wiki/Bird_strike "Bird strike") before landing at [Mumbai](/wiki/Chhatrapati_Shivaji_Maharaj_International_Airport "Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport").[221] While there were no injuries among passengers and crew, the plane suffered substantial damage and at least 36 flamingos were killed in the strike while the plane was flying over the [Ghatkopar](/wiki/Ghatkopar "Ghatkopar") suburban region of [Mumbai](/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"). An alternative aircraft was arranged for the return flight, and the plane was later repaired and put back into service.[222]
  * On 15 June 2024, Emirates Flight 262, an [Airbus A380-861](/wiki/Airbus_A380-800 "Airbus A380-800") registered as A6-EUL aborted the takeoff from runway 09L at [São Paulo/Guarulhos International Airport](/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo/Guarulhos_International_Airport "São Paulo/Guarulhos International Airport"), [SP](/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo "São Paulo") (GRU) after an engine on the left side surged while the flight crew applied takeoff thrust.[223]
  * On 25 September 2024, Emirates Flight 547, a [Boeing 777-300ER](/wiki/Boeing_777-300ER "Boeing 777-300ER") operating from [Chennai International Airport](/wiki/Chennai_International_Airport "Chennai International Airport") to [Dubai International Airport](/wiki/Dubai_International_Airport "Dubai International Airport"), began to emanate smoke when the refueling process was underway. Fire trucks were quickly rushed to the aircraft to extinguish the smoke. All passengers who were boarding were made to disembark the aircraft. The Dubai-bound flight was eventually delayed to 12:15 AM the next day.[224]
  * On October 20, 2025, [Emirates SkyCargo Flight 9788](/wiki/Emirates_SkyCargo_Flight_9788 "Emirates SkyCargo Flight 9788") operated by [Air ACT](/wiki/Air_ACT "Air ACT") suffered a runway excursion at [Hong Kong International Airport](/wiki/Hong_Kong_International_Airport "Hong Kong International Airport"), which then led to a collision with a ground vehicle. All 4 onboard the plane survived. 2 people on the ground were killed.



## Controversies

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Emirates_\(airline\)&action=edit&section=37 "Edit section: Controversies")]

See also: [Human rights in the United Arab Emirates](/wiki/Human_rights_in_the_United_Arab_Emirates "Human rights in the United Arab Emirates")

Emirates has received criticism for their treatment of staff, which Emirates has disputed and is declining year over year.[225] On September 23, 2016, an Italian man sued the airline after being squashed by an obese man for 9 hours.[226] In 2019, an Australian woman filed an unsuccessful lawsuit against Emirates for not providing her water, leading her to collapse during a long-haul flight.[227]

In August 2022, Emirates suspended its flights to Nigeria[228] after it got into a dispute with the government of Nigeria over the repatriation of an undisclosed amount of money from the country. The dispute has since been resolved and in June 2024, the airline resumed its regular flights to the West African nation.[229] Emirates has been sued twice in 2023 for deceptive advertising, once by a New Zealand male passenger and again two months later by a British energy trader.[230][231] The New Zealand case was awarded in favor of the passenger, and the British case is ongoing.[232][231][230]

In August 2023, a Pakistani man sued Emirates for [PKR](/wiki/Pakistani_rupee "Pakistani rupee") 5,000,000 claiming that the airline's service was extremely poor and below international standards.[233] On June 13, 2024, the [United States](/wiki/United_States "United States") government fined Emirates for $1.8 million for operating flights carrying JetBlue Airways' designator code below 32,000 feet (9,800 m) over prohibited airspace in [Iraq](/wiki/Iraq "Iraq").[234] On November 25, 2024, a Ghanaian businessman, Djanie Kotey filed a lawsuit against Emirates, but was dismissed.[235] On December 6, 2024, Emirates was sued by TAF Africas CEO Jake Epelle for [NGN](/wiki/Nigerian_naira "Nigerian naira") 150 million over human rights violations.[236]

Emirates is one of the few foreign airlines still serving Russia after major carriers pulled out of the country amid sweeping sanctions over the Ukraine war, leading to criticism as a result.[237][238]
