The **85th Academy Awards** ceremony, presented by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") (AMPAS), honored the best [films of 2012](/wiki/Films_of_2012 "Films of 2012") and took place on February 24, 2013, at the [Dolby Theatre](/wiki/Dolby_Theatre "Dolby Theatre") in [Hollywood, Los Angeles](/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles "Hollywood, Los Angeles"), beginning at 5:30 p.m. [Pacific Time Zone](/wiki/Pacific_Time_Zone "Pacific Time Zone") (PST) / 8:30 p.m. [Eastern Time Zone](/wiki/Eastern_Time_Zone "Eastern Time Zone") (EST). The ceremony was the first in the Academy's 85-year history to adopt the phrase "The Oscars" as the ceremony's official name during the broadcast and marketing.[7] During the ceremony, the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences presented [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") (commonly referred to as Oscars) in 24 categories. The ceremony was televised in the United States by [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company"), and produced by [Craig Zadan](/wiki/Craig_Zadan "Craig Zadan") and [Neil Meron](/wiki/Neil_Meron "Neil Meron") and directed by [Don Mischer](/wiki/Don_Mischer "Don Mischer").[8][9] Actor [Seth MacFarlane](/wiki/Seth_MacFarlane "Seth MacFarlane") hosted the show for the first time.[10]

In related events, the Academy held its 4th annual [Governors Awards](/wiki/Governors_Awards "Governors Awards") ceremony at the [Grand Ballroom](/wiki/Grand_Ballroom "Grand Ballroom") of the [Hollywood and Highland Center](/wiki/Hollywood_and_Highland_Center "Hollywood and Highland Center") on December 1, 2012.[11] On February 9, 2013, in a ceremony at [The Beverly Hills Hotel](/wiki/The_Beverly_Hills_Hotel "The Beverly Hills Hotel") in [Beverly Hills, California](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California "Beverly Hills, California"), the [Academy Awards for Technical Achievement](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Technical_Achievement "Academy Award for Technical Achievement") were presented by hosts [Chris Pine](/wiki/Chris_Pine "Chris Pine") and [Zoe Saldaña](/wiki/Zoe_Salda%C3%B1a "Zoe Saldaña").[12]

_[Argo](/wiki/Argo_\(2012_film\) "Argo \(2012 film\)")_ won three awards, including [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"), the first film to win an Academy Award for Best Picture without its director nominated since _[Driving Miss Daisy](/wiki/Driving_Miss_Daisy "Driving Miss Daisy")_.[13] Other winners included _[Life of Pi](/wiki/Life_of_Pi_\(film\) "Life of Pi \(film\)")_ with four awards, _[Les Misérables](/wiki/Les_Mis%C3%A9rables_\(2012_film\) "Les Misérables \(2012 film\)")_ with three, _[Django Unchained](/wiki/Django_Unchained "Django Unchained")_ , _[Lincoln](/wiki/Lincoln_\(film\) "Lincoln \(film\)")_ , and _[Skyfall](/wiki/Skyfall "Skyfall")_ with two, and _[Amour](/wiki/Amour_\(2012_film\) "Amour \(2012 film\)")_ , _[Anna Karenina](/wiki/Anna_Karenina_\(2012_film\) "Anna Karenina \(2012 film\)")_ , _[Brave](/wiki/Brave_\(2012_film\) "Brave \(2012 film\)")_ , _[Curfew](/wiki/Curfew_\(2012_film\) "Curfew \(2012 film\)")_ , _[Inocente](/wiki/Inocente "Inocente")_ , _[Paperman](/wiki/Paperman "Paperman")_ , _[Searching for Sugar Man](/wiki/Searching_for_Sugar_Man "Searching for Sugar Man")_ , _[Silver Linings Playbook](/wiki/Silver_Linings_Playbook "Silver Linings Playbook")_ and _[Zero Dark Thirty](/wiki/Zero_Dark_Thirty "Zero Dark Thirty")_ with one. The telecast garnered more than 40 million viewers in the United States. 

## Winners and nominees

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=85th_Academy_Awards&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Winners and nominees")]

The nominees for the 85th Academy Awards were announced on January 10, 2013, at 5:38 a.m. PST (13:38 [UTC](/wiki/UTC "UTC")) at the [Samuel Goldwyn Theater](/wiki/Samuel_Goldwyn_Theater "Samuel Goldwyn Theater") in [Beverly Hills, California](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California "Beverly Hills, California"), by [Seth MacFarlane](/wiki/Seth_MacFarlane "Seth MacFarlane"), host of the ceremony, and actress [Emma Stone](/wiki/Emma_Stone "Emma Stone").[14] _[Lincoln](/wiki/Lincoln_\(film\) "Lincoln \(film\)")_ received the most nominations with twelve total, and _[Life of Pi](/wiki/Life_of_Pi_\(film\) "Life of Pi \(film\)")_ came in second with eleven.[15]

The winners were announced during the awards ceremony on February 24, 2013.[16] _[Argo](/wiki/Argo_\(2012_film\) "Argo \(2012 film\)")_ was the fourth film to win [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture") without a directing nomination, following 1927's _[Wings](/wiki/Wings_\(1927_film\) "Wings \(1927 film\)")_ , 1932's _[Grand Hotel](/wiki/Grand_Hotel_\(1932_film\) "Grand Hotel \(1932 film\)")_ , and 1989's _[Driving Miss Daisy](/wiki/Driving_Miss_Daisy "Driving Miss Daisy")_.[17] As co-producer of _Argo_ , [George Clooney](/wiki/George_Clooney "George Clooney") became the third individual to win Oscars for both acting and producing.[18] By virtue of his nomination for [Best Original Song](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Academy Award for Best Original Song") in _[Ted](/wiki/Ted_\(film\) "Ted \(film\)")_ , host MacFarlane became the sixth person since [James Franco](/wiki/James_Franco "James Franco"), who was a co-host and a Best Actor nominee during the [83rd ceremony](/wiki/83rd_Academy_Awards "83rd Academy Awards") in 2011, to host the ceremony while receiving a nomination in the same year.[19][20] _[Silver Linings Playbook](/wiki/Silver_Linings_Playbook "Silver Linings Playbook")_ was the fourteenth film to earn [nominations in all four acting categories](/wiki/List_of_films_with_all_four_Academy_Award_acting_nominations "List of films with all four Academy Award acting nominations"), and the first since _[Reds](/wiki/Reds_\(film\) "Reds \(film\)")_ in [1981](/wiki/54th_Academy_Awards "54th Academy Awards").[21] At age 22, Best Actress winner [Jennifer Lawrence](/wiki/Jennifer_Lawrence "Jennifer Lawrence") became the second-youngest winner in that category.[22] With his third win for [Best Lead Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor"), [Daniel Day-Lewis](/wiki/Daniel_Day-Lewis "Daniel Day-Lewis") became the first three-time winner in that category.[23] He also was the sixth performer to win at least three acting Oscars.[24] _[Amour](/wiki/Amour_\(2012_film\) "Amour \(2012 film\)")_ was the fourth film nominated[25] simultaneously for Best Picture and [Best Foreign Language Film](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Foreign_Language_Film "Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film") in the same year.[26] At age nine, [Quvenzhané Wallis](/wiki/Quvenzhan%C3%A9_Wallis "Quvenzhané Wallis") became the youngest nominee for [Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress") and the youngest female acting nominee overall.[21] Meanwhile, [Emmanuelle Riva](/wiki/Emmanuelle_Riva "Emmanuelle Riva") (aged 85) was the oldest nominee for Best Actress.[27] This marked the first time in Oscar history that all five nominees in an acting category (Best Supporting Actor) were all previous winners.[28] _[Skyfall](/wiki/Skyfall "Skyfall")_ and _[Zero Dark Thirty](/wiki/Zero_Dark_Thirty "Zero Dark Thirty")_ 's joint win in the [Best Sound Editing](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Sound_Editing "Academy Award for Best Sound Editing") category was the sixth occurrence of a tie in Oscar history.[29] _Skyfall_ won the award for [Best Original Song](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Academy Award for Best Original Song"), becoming the first [Bond song](/wiki/James_Bond_music "James Bond music") to win the award.

### Governors Awards

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=85th_Academy_Awards&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Governors Awards")]

The Academy held its 4th Annual [Governors Awards](/wiki/Governors_Awards "Governors Awards") ceremony on December 1, 2012, during which the following awards were presented:[32][33][34]

#### Honorary Awards

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=85th_Academy_Awards&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Honorary Awards")]

  * To [Hal Needham](/wiki/Hal_Needham "Hal Needham"), an innovator, mentor, and master technician who elevated his craft to an art and made the impossible look easy.[35]
  * To [D. A. Pennebaker](/wiki/D._A._Pennebaker "D. A. Pennebaker"), who redefined the language of film and taught a generation of filmmakers to look to reality for inspiration.[35]
  * To [George Stevens, Jr.](/wiki/George_Stevens,_Jr. "George Stevens, Jr."), a tireless champion of the arts in America and especially that most American of arts: the Hollywood film.[35]



#### Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=85th_Academy_Awards&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award")]

  * [Jeffrey Katzenberg](/wiki/Jeffrey_Katzenberg "Jeffrey Katzenberg") — who has led our community in enlightened philanthropy by his extraordinary example.[35]

## Ceremony information

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=85th_Academy_Awards&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Ceremony information")]

[](/wiki/File:Seth_MacFarlane_2012_cropped_and_retouched.jpg)[Seth MacFarlane](/wiki/Seth_MacFarlane "Seth MacFarlane") hosted the 85th Academy Awards

Due to declining interest and viewership in recent ceremonies, the Academy hired a new production team in an attempt to improve ratings and revive interest in the ceremony. Reports surfaced that Academy then-president [Tom Sherak](/wiki/Tom_Sherak "Tom Sherak") approached television producer [Lorne Michaels](/wiki/Lorne_Michaels "Lorne Michaels") for producing duties with actor and comedian [Jimmy Fallon](/wiki/Jimmy_Fallon "Jimmy Fallon") as host.[39] However, the telecast's broadcaster [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") objected to these selections, and both men declined afterward.[40] With newly elected Academy president [Hawk Koch](/wiki/Hawk_Koch "Hawk Koch") assuming leadership duties, the Academy hired [Neil Meron](/wiki/Neil_Meron "Neil Meron") and [Craig Zadan](/wiki/Craig_Zadan "Craig Zadan") in August 2012 to produce the ceremony. Two months later, the Academy announced that actor, director, animator, singer, and comedian [Seth MacFarlane](/wiki/Seth_MacFarlane "Seth MacFarlane") would host the telecast.[41] MacFarlane expressed that it was truly an honor and a thrill to be asked to host Academy Awards commenting, "It's truly an overwhelming privilege to be asked to host the Oscars. My thoughts upon hearing the news were, one, I will do my utmost to live up to the high standards set forth by my predecessors; and two, I hope they don't find out I hosted the [Charlie Sheen Roast](/wiki/Comedy_Central_Roast "Comedy Central Roast")."[42] In an unusual break from previous years, producers Meron and Zadan announced that the on-air telecast of the ceremony would be simply referred to as "The Oscars" instead of "The 85th Annual Academy Awards".[7]

As evident by the numerous musical numbers featured throughout the telecast, the ceremony was billed as a salute to music and the movies.[43] In keeping with the theme of the evening, numerous [film scores](/wiki/Film_score "Film score") from various motion pictures were played intermittently throughout the ceremony; most notable was [John Williams](/wiki/John_Williams "John Williams")' theme music from _[Jaws](/wiki/Jaws_\(film\) "Jaws \(film\)")_ , which was used to goad winners off the stage if their acceptance speeches were overly long.[44][45] In a departure from having the orchestra perform in the same theatre, composer [Williams Ross](/wiki/William_Ross_\(composer\) "William Ross \(composer\)") conducted the orchestra from a studio inside the [Capitol Records Building](/wiki/Capitol_Records_Building "Capitol Records Building") a mile away.[46]

Several other people were involved with the telecast and its promotion. [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Award "Tony Award")-winning art director [Derek McLane](/wiki/Derek_McLane "Derek McLane") designed a new set and stage design for the ceremony.[47] [Rob Ashford](/wiki/Rob_Ashford "Rob Ashford") served as choreographer for several musical numbers during the event.[48] Comedians [Ben Gleib](/wiki/Ben_Gleib "Ben Gleib") and Annie Greenup served as correspondents and hosts of "Oscar Road Trip", a nationwide bus tour promoting the ceremony in eleven major cities across the United States.[49] Six young film students from colleges across the country, who were selected from a contest conducted by AMPAS and [MtvU](/wiki/MtvU "MtvU"), were recruited to appear onstage to deliver Oscar statuettes to the presenters during the gala.[50]

### Introduction of electronic voting system

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=85th_Academy_Awards&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Introduction of electronic voting system")]

In January 2012, AMPAS announced that it would create electronic voting system starting with the 2013 ceremony as another method for members to select the nominees and winners during the process.[51] According to AMPAS Chief Operating Officer Ric Robertson, the implementation of the digital ballot was designed to increase participation among members in the voting process and to provide an alternative method of voting in case of emergency.[51] Despite several Academy officials denying such reasons, some industry insiders speculated that the introduction of electronic voting was another move toward moving future awards galas to January.[52] The deadline to submit nomination ballots was originally scheduled for January 3, but technological errors and glitches prompted the Academy to move the deadline one day later.[53]

### Box office performance of nominated films

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=85th_Academy_Awards&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Box office performance of nominated films")]

None of the nine Best Picture nominees were among the top ten releases in box office during the nominations. However, four of those films had already earned $100 million in American and Canadian ticket sales.[54] At the time of the announcement of nominations on January 10, _Lincoln_ was the highest-grossing film among the Best Picture nominees with $144 million in domestic box office receipts. The other three films to earn $100 million prior to nominations were _Django Unchained_ with $112 million, _Argo_ with $110 million, and _Les Misérables_ with $103 million. Among the five remaining Best Picture nominees, _Life of Pi_ was the next highest-grossing film with $91.8 million followed by _Silver Linings Playbook_ ($35.7 million), _Beasts of the Southern Wild_ ($11.2 million), _Zero Dark Thirty_ ($4.4 million), and finally _Amour_ ($311,247).[B][55] The combined gross of the nine Best Picture nominees when the Oscars were announced was $620 million with an average gross of $68.9 million per film.[55]

Of the top 50 grossing movies of the year, 61 nominations went to 15 films on the list. Only _Brave_ (8th), _Wreck-It Ralph_ (13th), _Lincoln_ (17th), _Django Unchained_ (23rd), _Argo_ (26th), _Les Misérables_ (27th), _Flight_ (30th), and _Life of Pi_ (31st) were nominated for Best Picture, Best Animated Feature, or any of the directing, acting, or screenwriting awards.[56] The other top 50 box office hits that earned nominations were _Marvel's The Avengers_ (1st), _Skyfall_ (4th), _The Hobbit: An Unexpected Journey_ (6th), _Ted_ (13th), _Snow White and the Huntsman_ (15th), _Prometheus_ (20th), and _Mirror Mirror_ (44th).[56]

### "We Saw Your Boobs" controversy

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=85th_Academy_Awards&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: "We Saw Your Boobs" controversy")]

During the opening monologue, MacFarlane is told by [James T. Kirk](/wiki/James_T._Kirk "James T. Kirk") ([William Shatner](/wiki/William_Shatner "William Shatner")) (Captain Kirk set in the next day) about how he was going to ruin the telecast, Captain Kirk then shows him a music video where MacFarlane sings _We Saw Your Boobs_. Its lyrics lists out movies that featured scenes of actresses' disrobing.[57]

The song has mixed reviews. On the positive side, _[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ reported, "MacFarlane was employed partly to puncture the event's pomposity, which he did by lightheartedly pointing out that some of the world's most [self-important](/wiki/Egotism "Egotism") people regularly get their kit off for money".[58] [SheKnows](/wiki/SheKnows "SheKnows") wondered if the live reaction of some of the actresses were indeed acting as the "pre-recorded spoof apparently looked real enough for social media to worry about it".[59] On the negative side, actress [Jane Fonda](/wiki/Jane_Fonda "Jane Fonda") stated, "if they want to stoop to that, why not list all the [penises](/wiki/Penis "Penis") we've seen? Better yet, remember that this is a telecast seen around the world watched by families with their children and to many this is neither appropriate or funny."[60] California assemblywoman [Bonnie Lowenthal](/wiki/Bonnie_Lowenthal "Bonnie Lowenthal") and state senator [Hannah-Beth Jackson](/wiki/Hannah-Beth_Jackson "Hannah-Beth Jackson") expressed their disappointment at MacFarlane, ABC, and AMPAS in a press release reading, "there was a disturbing theme about violence against women being acceptable and funny. From topical jabs about domestic violence to singing about 'boobs' during a film's rape scene, Seth MacFarlane crossed the line from [humor](/wiki/Humor "Humor") to [misogyny](/wiki/Misogyny "Misogyny")."[61] [Amy Davidson](/wiki/Amy_Davidson_\(author\) "Amy Davidson \(author\)") of _[The New Yorker](/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")_ interpreted the song as hostile to women.[62]

In a press release statement, the Academy defended MacFarlane for expressing his [artistic freedom](/wiki/Artistic_freedom "Artistic freedom"), "If the Oscars are about anything, they're about creative freedom. We think the show's producers Craig Zadan and Neil Meron, and host Seth MacFarlane did a great job and we hope our worldwide audience found the show entertaining."[63]

### Critical reviews

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=85th_Academy_Awards&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Critical reviews")]

The show received a mixed reception from media publications. Some media outlets were more critical of the show. Columnist [Owen Gleiberman](/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman "Owen Gleiberman") of _[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_ commented "By calling constant attention to the naughty factor", MacFarlane created "an echo chamber of outrage, working a little too hard to top himself with faux-scandalous gags about race, Jews in Hollywood, and the killing of Abraham Lincoln."[64] _[The Washington Post](/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")_ television critic [Hank Stuever](/wiki/Hank_Stuever "Hank Stuever") bemoaned, "There was nothing notably terrible about the show, and nothing particularly enthralling." Regarding MacFarlane's performance as host, Stuever noted, "What you got was a combination of sicko and retro, an Oscar show hosted by someone who waited until Oscar night to discover that he's only so-so at stand-up comedy."[65] Television editor [Alan Sepinwall](/wiki/Alan_Sepinwall "Alan Sepinwall") of [HitFix](/wiki/HitFix "HitFix") lamented that the ceremony made for a "frequently messy, but occasionally surprising and/or entertaining evening." He added that MacFarlane "had some funny moments here and there, but he missed way more than he hit, and Frat Boy Seth quickly assumed dominance as the evening went along."[66]

Other media outlets received the broadcast and more positively. Tim Goodman of _[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")_ praised MacFarlane's performance saying that he did "impressively better than one would have wagered." He also noted that he added "plenty of niceties with a little bit of the [Ricky Gervais](/wiki/Ricky_Gervais "Ricky Gervais") bite-the-hand-that-feeds-you thing and worked the juxtaposition rather nicely.[67] _[Chicago Tribune](/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")_ television critic Nina Metz lauded MacFarlane for keeping "a solid handle on the proceedings." She also remarked that the host "opened with a series of jokes that were bona fide winners, landing on just the right tone: confident but not cocksure".[68] [Associated Press](/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press") critic [Frazier Moore](/wiki/Frazier_Moore "Frazier Moore") extolled MacFarlane observing that he "seized the camera Sunday as host of ABC's Oscarcast and proved to its vast audience that he's a ridiculously versatile entertainer, a guy who can be as charming as he is famously irreverent, even polarizing."[69]

### Ratings and reception

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=85th_Academy_Awards&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Ratings and reception")]

The American telecast on ABC drew in an average of 40.38 million people over its length, which was a 3% increase from the previous year's ceremony.[6] An estimated 77.92 million total viewers watched all or part of the awards.[70] The show also drew higher [Nielsen ratings](/wiki/Nielsen_ratings "Nielsen ratings") compared to the two previous ceremonies with 24.47% of households watching over a 35.65 share.[71] In addition, the program scored its highest key demo ratings in six years with a 13.71 rating over a 33.45 share among viewers in the 18–49 demographic.[72]

In July 2013, the ceremony presentation received nine nominations for the [65th Primetime Emmys](/wiki/65th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards "65th Primetime Emmy Awards").[73] The following month, the ceremony didn't win any of the nominations.[74]
