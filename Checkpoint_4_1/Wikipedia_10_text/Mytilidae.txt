The **Mytilidae** are a [family](/wiki/Family_\(biology\) "Family \(biology\)") of small to large [marine](/wiki/Marine_life "Marine life") and [brackish-water](/wiki/Brackish_water "Brackish water") [bivalve](/wiki/Bivalve "Bivalve") [molluscs](/wiki/Mollusc "Mollusc") in the [order](/wiki/Order_\(biology\) "Order \(biology\)") [Mytilida](/wiki/Mytilida "Mytilida"). One of the [genera](/wiki/Genera "Genera"), _[Limnoperna](/wiki/Limnoperna_fortunei "Limnoperna fortunei")_ , inhabits [freshwater](/wiki/Fresh_water "Fresh water") environments. Mytilidae, which contains some 52 genera, is the only extant family within the order Mytilida.[1]

Species in the family Mytilidae are found worldwide, but they are more abundant in colder seas, where they often form uninterrupted beds on rocky shores in the [intertidal zone](/wiki/Intertidal_zone "Intertidal zone") and the shallow subtidal. The subfamily Bathymodiolinae is found in [deep-sea](/wiki/Deep-sea "Deep-sea") habitats. 

Mytilids include the well-known, edible sea mussels. 

A common feature of the shells of mussels is an asymmetrical shell, which has a thick, adherent [periostracum](/wiki/Periostracum "Periostracum"). The animals attach themselves to a solid substrate using a [byssus](/wiki/Byssus "Byssus"). 

A 2020 study of the phylogeny of the Mytilidae recovered two main [clades](/wiki/Clade "Clade") derived from an [epifaunal](/wiki/Epifaunal "Epifaunal") ancestor, with subsequent lineages shifting to other lifestyles, and correlating [convergent evolution](/wiki/Convergent_evolution "Convergent evolution") of [siphon](/wiki/Siphon "Siphon") traits.[2]
